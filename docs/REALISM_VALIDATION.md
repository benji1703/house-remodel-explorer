# 3D landscape realism validation

Validation pass: 2026-09-13. Reference: `public/references/plants-beit-hananiah-hero.webp`.

## Confirmed cause of the green shapes

The large flat green ovals visible around the plants are generated by `PlantingBeds` in `components/landscape/MediterraneanLandscape.tsx`, specifically the `BED_EDGE` material (`#77815d`) on flattened sphere geometries. They are presentation geometry, not real lawn, planting soil, or a measured site surface. Their regular elliptical outlines and uniform green colour are the main reason the overview reads as a diagram / legacy game scene.

The dark brown areas are the `BED` material (`#4d4334`) using the same flattened sphere shapes. They also read as isolated floating islands because there is no irregular soil edge, mulch transition, stones, leaf litter, or continuous grade transition into the gravel.

## Reference comparison

The hero image establishes a close, low garden view with:

- continuous warm limestone / compacted gravel circulation;
- irregular planted drifts with overlapping mature canopies, rather than separated oval islands;
- dark soil visible only inside soft planting pockets;
- no saturated green lawn discs or hard graphic borders;
- warm late-afternoon light, contact occlusion, and atmospheric background planting;
- a restrained material palette: travertine, dusty olive foliage, blue-grey herbs, straw grasses, and magenta bougainvillea.

## Blocking visual gaps in the current overview

1. **Green discs / clipped islands (P0).** Remove `BED_EDGE` entirely for this reference mode. Replace each planting pocket with irregular, low-profile soil/mulch geometry whose perimeter is hidden by foliage and stones. If a green groundcover is desired, use a texture-driven patch with varied albedo and no perfect ellipse.
2. **Top-down camera (P0).** The overview is a plan inspection angle, not the hero composition. Add a reference camera around 1.5–1.7 m eye height, 28–40 mm equivalent lens, looking horizontally through foreground planting toward the pergola and stone facade. Keep overview available for geometry audit.
3. **Ground is too uniform (P0).** The gravel plane is a single uninterrupted tile. Break it with irregular seams, tonal variation, pebble scale variation, and a clearly modeled/texture-driven compacted path. Keep paths continuous to doors and stairs.
4. **Plant spacing is grid-like (P1).** The `drifts` generator uses repeated spacing and two rows. Introduce jitter in position, scale, and yaw; group plants in 3–7 specimen clusters with deliberate height layering and overlap. Maintain the clearance predicates.
5. **Lighting/material response (P1).** The current neutral presentation mode makes plaster and ground grey. For the hero profile use warm sun, cooler sky fill, contact shadows under foliage, and physically varied roughness. Avoid green tint on walls or ground.
6. **Background/site termination (P1).** The square 19 × 17.5 m presentation ground and visible empty beige beyond it expose the render boundary. Hide or soften the edge with a larger matte terrain/backdrop and atmospheric depth, without treating it as approved site geometry.

## Revalidation after bed and lighting edits — 2026-09-13

The bed rewrite removes the old green cookie-cutter discs and uses irregular polygon outlines with a dark bed and lighter mulch inset. This addresses the most obvious defect, but the current implementation still has three photographic mismatches to resolve:

- **Pale grey substrate remains visible.** `Gravel` still lays a large uniform `#c8b99f` tiled plane over the scene, with only a single texture repeat and no transition into the bed polygons. In the supplied overview this reads as pale grey concrete or a blank BIM substrate. The hero reads as warm, granular limestone gravel with an irregular compacted path. Add color/roughness breakup at a larger scale, and mask the gravel around beds so soil/mulch meets it softly.
- **Blue cast is now likely too strong.** The updated sky and hemisphere use blue values (`#73a6d5`, `#b9d4e8`, `#a9c2e0`) while the house and substrate remain pale. This combination can push the plaster, gravel, and foliage toward a cool grey cast. Keep blue in the upper sky only; reduce blue fill on the ground-facing hemisphere and retain warm reflected bounce from the sand/stone environment. Judge at the garden camera during the golden-hour setting.
- **Density is still sparse at overview scale.** The reference has a near-continuous foreground tapestry and overlapping midground layers. The current fixed drift counts plus clearance filtering leave broad empty substrate, especially in front of the house. Increase cluster overlap and add low foreground fillers (rosemary, sage, sea lavender, lomandra) while preserving `isPlantClear`; avoid evenly spaced rows.

The revised outlines are an improvement, but acceptance still requires a low eye-level garden render. The top-down overview remains useful for geometry validation and should not be used as the hero comparison frame.
